# SWEBOK — Software Engineering Management

This skill grounds work in the IEEE **SWEBOK Guide v4.0**, Knowledge Area 9 (*Software Engineering Management*): planning, coordinating, measuring, monitoring, controlling, and reporting to ensure software is developed and maintained effectively.
Distill the guide into actionable checklists — never paste large verbatim excerpts of the IEEE text.
Cite it as "SWEBOK v4, KA Software Engineering Management, §<n>" when the user wants provenance.

## Pick a mode

This KA is decision-heavy. Default to **Advise** — answer questions from the vocabulary and topic map below. Use `references/artifacts.md` when the user wants a concrete plan/record produced, and `references/review-checklist.md` when auditing an existing plan/decision.

## Core vocabulary (use it precisely)

Management proceeds through **initiation & scope definition → planning → execution → review & evaluation → closure**, with **measurement** underpinning all of it.

Planning covers **effort/schedule/cost estimation**, **resource allocation**, **risk management**, **quality management**, and plan management. Execution adds supplier/contract management and the measurement process (monitor → control → report).

## Topic map (SWEBOK v4, KA 9)

Use this as the outline for generating complete artifacts and for auditing coverage in a review.

1. Initiation & scope definition — determination & negotiation of requirements; feasibility analysis; review/revision of requirements.
2. Planning — process planning; deliverables; effort/schedule/cost estimation; resource allocation; risk management; quality management; plan management.
3. Execution — implementing plans; acquisition & supplier contract management; measurement process; monitor; control; reporting.
4. Review & evaluation — satisfaction of requirements; reviewing performance.
5. Closure — determining closure; closure activities.
6. Measurement — establish/sustain commitment; plan; perform; evaluate measurement.
7. Tools.

## Mode: Advise

- Anchor estimates in a **method** (analogy, parametric, bottom-up) and express **ranges + assumptions**, not single false-precision numbers.
- Run **risk management** continuously: identify → analyze (prob×impact) → plan response → monitor; keep a living risk register.
- Define a small set of **measures tied to decisions** (GQM); don't measure what you won't act on.
- Make **scope, schedule, cost, quality** trade-offs explicit — you can't fix all four.
